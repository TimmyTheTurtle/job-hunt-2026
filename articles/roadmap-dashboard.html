Series 1 — The Vibe Coding Problem

Audience: engineering leaders, senior engineers, CTOs. Platform: LinkedIn primary, personal site canonical. Cadence: one article every 10–14 days.

The central thesis: vibe coding reproduces the engagement dynamics of social media feeds. The argument runs ten articles from diagnosis through architecture — each article justifies the next. Do not publish out of order.

Series 2 — AI Systems Engineering

Audience: senior engineers, technical leads, AI practitioners. Platform: personal site primary. No fixed cadence — write in parallel with Series 1, publish when ready.

The technical counterpart to Series 1. Where S1 diagnoses the problem, S2 names the engineering disciplines that address it. TDD, V-model, context architecture, SDLC separation, adversarial testing.

Series 3 — Product Discovery in the AI Age

Audience: product engineers, technical founders, engineering leads. Platform: LinkedIn and personal site. Cross-links to Series 1 and 2.

All three articles draw from the Watershed exploration phase, which is complete. They can be written without waiting for Watershed to reach Phase A. Write these this week — no blockers.

Series 4 — Legal Tech Debt

Audience: insurance carriers, legal tech practitioners, RegTech engineers, computational law researchers. Building toward a peer-reviewed paper. Articles are excerpts and proof-of-concept publications.

All examples are synthetic. Real corpus analysis informs the taxonomy; real policy text is never published. Primary sources: peer-reviewed academic work, court records, regulatory filings, NAIC data. The identities of the guilty parties have been protected.

Build items B1–B10

Project work that unlocks blocked articles. Critical path items gate first outreach at week 6. Long-game items gate Series 1 late articles and S2-A5.
