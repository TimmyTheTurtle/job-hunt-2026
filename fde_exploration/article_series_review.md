# Article Series Review Through An FDE Lens

Date: 2026-07-23

## Bottom Line

The current article program is strong for building long-term technical identity, but it is not
yet optimized for helping an FDE or AI solutions employer quickly understand Dorian's value.

The strongest existing series for FDE relevance are:

- Series 1 - because it shows skepticism, operating discipline, and engineering realism
- Series 2 - because it frames AI systems as architecture, workflow, boundaries, and quality
- Series 4 - because it grounds the work in domain pain, document complexity, and measurable cost

The weakest fit for immediate FDE signaling is not quality. It is distance from field reality.
The current writing often reads like architecture doctrine, not like "I have seen broken customer
workflows and know how to make them usable."

That does not mean the current material is wrong. It means the first-island FDE strategy needs an
adjacent article package with a different entry point.

## What The Current Article Program Already Does Well

- Treats AI as a systems problem rather than a prompt trick
- Emphasizes constraints, quality gates, evidence, and human review
- Builds credibility around traceability, retrieval, memory architecture, and bounded autonomy
- Supports long-term differentiation from generic AI builders
- Connects well to legal-tech-debt and document-heavy workflows

## What It Under-Signals For FDE

- customer reality
- deployment friction
- implementation tradeoffs under time pressure
- integration pain across tools and systems
- adoption blockers
- technical discovery in ambiguous environments
- the practical bridge between "good architecture" and "customer got value this month"

An FDE hiring manager is more likely to respond quickly to:

- implementation stories
- workflow rescue stories
- system-boundary decisions in production-like situations
- human adoption problems
- migration, onboarding, or debugging insights

## Recommended Editorial Shift For The FDE Island

Keep the technical spine, but change the lead question.

Current lead question:

> What does rigorous AI systems engineering look like?

FDE-island lead question:

> What does it take to make AI workflows work inside messy real organizations?

That shift still allows:

- retrieval
- context architecture
- bounded autonomy
- evidence bundles
- audit trails
- human review gates

But the framing becomes more field-oriented and easier for employers to map to deployed work.

## What To Preserve

Do not discard:

- the anti-hype voice
- the systems-engineering discipline
- the evidence-first mindset
- the document and workflow focus
- the token-frugality / context-architecture ideas

These are differentiators.

## What To Add

Add more writing that sounds like:

- implementation notes from the field
- what breaks during deployment
- why customer workflows fail AI demos
- how to place deterministic boundaries around model behavior
- why human review is a workflow design problem, not only a model-quality problem
- what counts as "production enough" for an AI-assisted workflow
