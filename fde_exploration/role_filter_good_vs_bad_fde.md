# Good FDE vs Bad FDE Filter

Date: 2026-07-23

Use this before investing in an FDE-shaped role.

## Good FDE For Dorian

Green flags:

- hands-on coding is explicit, not implied
- implementation, integration, debugging, or deployment ownership is core to the role
- embedded customer work feeds product and engineering learning
- success means shipped workflows or live deployments, not just happy accounts
- AI workflow, automation, document handling, or operational tooling is part of the real work
- the role values ambiguity tolerance and technical ownership
- the employer wants someone who can define structure where none exists
- there is real room to build repeatable assets, playbooks, or product improvements
- travel is moderate and purposeful rather than constant fire-fighting
- the role strengthens future positioning toward applied AI systems or durable workflow software

## Bad FDE For Dorian

Red flags:

- job reads like customer success with occasional scripting
- job reads like pre-sales demos with little post-deployment ownership
- "solutions" means slide decks, workshops, and stakeholder coordination without real code
- the engineer is mostly an account smoother or escalation sponge
- success metrics are adoption, renewal, and expansion with little mention of implementation depth
- the role requires heavy travel without strong technical upside
- there is weak product feedback loop and little reusable engineering output
- no evidence of serious engineering culture, debugging depth, or system ownership
- "AI" is mostly marketing language over lightweight automation

## Fast Scoring Rubric

Score 1 to 5 on each:

- Coding depth
- Integration and deployment ownership
- Product and engineering feedback loop
- AI/workflow relevance
- Narrative value for the next role
- Compensation / runway value
- Energy cost

Interpretation:

- 28 to 35: strong FDE target
- 22 to 27: good pragmatic target
- 16 to 21: situational target
- 15 or below: weak target unless money/runway is unusually strong

## Safe Interview Questions

- How much of the role is hands-on implementation versus discovery, enablement, or account management?
- What does a successful first 90 days look like in shipped work?
- Does the role mainly customize deployments, build net-new workflows, or support adoption?
- How often does work from this team become reusable product capability?
- What percentage of time is customer-facing meetings versus code, debugging, or implementation?
- How much travel is typical in practice, not just on paper?

## Default Rule

The ideal FDE role should feel like:

> engineer first, field operator second, account function third.

If that ordering is reversed, the role is probably a bad fit.
