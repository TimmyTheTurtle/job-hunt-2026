# COVER LETTER RULES FOR CODEX

## Objective

Write cover letters that are truthful, tailored, tactically useful, and aligned with Dorian's broader strategy.

## Required qualities

Every cover letter should be:
- specific to the role
- factually grounded
- professional
- concise
- technically credible
- calm in tone
- intentional rather than emotional

## Tone

Target tone:
- intelligent
- direct
- composed
- serious
- curious
- polished without sounding overproduced

Avoid tone that is:
- desperate
- overexcited
- worshipful
- defensive
- generic
- salesy
- melodramatic

## Structure

Recommended structure:
1. opening: identify role and immediate fit
2. middle: connect 2-3 relevant pieces of background to the company's needs
3. middle: explain why the work is interesting and directionally aligned
4. close: express interest in further discussion

## Safe narrative themes

Use one or more of these themes:

### 1. Systems and delivery

Dorian is strongest when framed as someone who turns messy requirements into working systems.

### 2. Practical builder

He has built real software in real operating environments, not just coursework or toy demos.

### 3. Business-aware engineer

He understands both software and operational/business realities.

### 4. Deliberate technical growth

He is intentionally growing toward deeper systems, C++, and technical rigor.

## What to emphasize by role type

### Systems / C++ / simulation roles

Emphasize:
- systems thinking
- debugging
- technical rigor
- performance awareness
- active work in modern C++ and simulation-oriented projects

### Implementation / solutions / consulting roles

Emphasize:
- client/problem translation
- ownership
- practical delivery
- cross-functional communication
- business + technical fluency

### Business software / platform roles

Emphasize:
- workflow design
- configuration logic
- operational software
- maintainability
- correctness

### Analytics / reporting / compliance-oriented roles

Emphasize:
- structured reasoning
- detail orientation
- handling ambiguity
- producing reliable outputs from messy requirements

## Forbidden content

Do not mention:
- unemployment
- UI compliance
- desperation for income
- starting a business later, unless directly relevant
- resentment toward the market
- exaggerated expertise

## Avoid these cliches

- passionate self-starter rockstar
- dream company
- ever since I was a child
- perfect fit
- hit the ground running in every context
- think outside the box
- synergistic

## Good opening patterns

- I'm interested in this role because it sits at the intersection of structured engineering, practical delivery, and the kind of systems-oriented work I'm intentionally moving toward.
- My background combines hands-on software delivery with a strong systems mindset, and this role stands out because it appears to value both technical rigor and practical problem solving.
- I'm drawn to roles where careful engineering and clear thinking matter, particularly when the work involves turning complex requirements into reliable software.

## Good closing patterns

- I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my background could support your team.
- I'd be glad to speak further about how my experience aligns with the needs of this role.
- Thank you for your consideration. I'd be pleased to discuss the position in more detail.

## Length guidance

Default target:
- 250 to 400 words

Shorter is better when:
- application form already contains detailed answers
- employer is likely screening quickly

Longer is acceptable when:
- the role is especially aligned
- there is a meaningful narrative bridge to explain

## Final check before output

Before finalizing, verify:
1. Does this sound like a real person?
2. Is it tailored to the actual role?
3. Are all claims defensible?
4. Does it reinforce a coherent career direction?
5. Is there any fluff that should be cut?
